FHA loans themselves are a standardized federal program, but the lenders
offering them are not standardized at all. Fees, overlays, communication,
and timelines vary a lot from one lender to the next — so "which FHA
lender is best" really comes down to how to compare them, not a single
name that fits every buyer.

## Compare APR, not just the advertised rate

The interest rate alone doesn't tell you the full cost of the loan.
**APR** folds in lender fees and points, giving you a more apples-to-apples
number when comparing quotes. Two lenders advertising the same interest
rate can have meaningfully different APRs once fees are factored in.

## Ask about "overlays"

FHA sets baseline requirements — but individual lenders are allowed to
add their own stricter standards on top, called **overlays**. A lender
might require a higher minimum credit score or lower debt-to-income
ratio than FHA itself does. If you're near FHA's baseline qualifying
range, this is worth asking about directly, since it can be the
difference between an approval and a decline at a specific lender.

## Ask about experience pairing with MSHDA

If you're planning to combine an FHA loan with MSHDA down payment
assistance, not every lender participates in MSHDA's program, and
experience level with that pairing varies. A lender who handles MSHDA
combinations regularly is likely to move through that part of the
process more smoothly than one doing it rarely.

## Get quotes the same day, in writing

Rates move daily, so comparing a quote from Monday against one from
Thursday isn't a fair comparison. Getting written Loan Estimates from a
few lenders on the same day gives you a genuinely comparable picture.

## Weigh service and timeline, not just cost

The lowest-cost quote isn't automatically the right choice if that
lender has a track record of slow communication or missed timelines —
especially under a tight purchase contract deadline. Ask about typical
turn times for underwriting and closing, and consider asking your real
estate agent which lenders they've seen perform reliably on recent
Michigan closings.

## A simple comparison checklist

- Interest rate and APR, quoted the same day
- Lender fees / origination charges
- Any credit score or DTI overlays above FHA's own minimums
- Experience combining FHA with MSHDA assistance, if relevant to you
- Estimated closing timeline
- How responsive they were during the quote process itself — often a
  preview of what working together will be like

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the lender with the lowest rate always the best choice?**
Not necessarily — a low rate paired with high fees, a slow process, or
an overlay that puts your approval at risk isn't automatically the best
deal. It's worth weighing the full picture.

**Do all lenders offer FHA loans?**
No — FHA loans require specific lender approval to originate, so not
every lender offers them. It's worth confirming a lender is
FHA-approved before requesting a quote.

**How many lenders should I get quotes from?**
There's no fixed number, but comparing at least two or three gives you
a meaningfully better sense of the range of rates, fees, and service
than relying on a single quote.
