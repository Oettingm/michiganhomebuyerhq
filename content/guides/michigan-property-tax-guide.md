Michigan's property tax system trips up a lot of buyers, mainly because of
one specific rule: **taxes don't automatically reset to match your
purchase price**, but they also don't stay identical to what the seller
was paying either. Here's what's actually going on.

## Taxable value vs. assessed value

Michigan tracks two different numbers for every property:

- **Assessed value**: roughly half the property's market value, updated
  annually by the local assessor
- **Taxable value**: the number your tax bill is actually based on, which
  is capped from rising more than the rate of inflation (or 5%, whichever
  is lower) each year — as long as ownership doesn't change

## Why this matters when you buy

When a property sells, its taxable value is **uncapped** and reset to
match the assessed value the following tax year. In practice, this often
means your tax bill goes up compared to what the seller was paying,
especially if they'd owned the home a long time and benefited from years
of the cap holding their taxable value down.

This is one of the most common surprises for first-time buyers — the
listing's tax figure or the seller's current bill isn't a reliable
predictor of what you'll actually owe.

## Millage rates vary by location

On top of the taxable value calculation, the actual tax rate (called the
**millage rate**) is set locally — by your county, city or township,
school district, and any local millages voters have approved. This means
two similarly priced homes in different Michigan cities can have
meaningfully different tax bills.

## Principal Residence Exemption (PRE)

If the home will be your primary residence, you can file for Michigan's
**Principal Residence Exemption**, which removes the local school
operating tax portion from your bill — a meaningful reduction. This isn't
automatic; you generally need to file the form with your local assessor
after closing.

## What this means for your budget

Because of the uncapping rule, it's safer to estimate your property tax
based on the **sale price and local millage rate**, not the seller's
current tax bill. Your lender's estimate at pre-approval should already
account for this, but it's worth double-checking rather than assuming the
listing sheet number is accurate for you.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will my property taxes stay the same every year after I buy?**
After the reset in the year following your purchase, your taxable value
is capped again going forward, so increases should be modest and
inflation-linked rather than jumping again — unless you sell, at which
point it uncaps for the next buyer.

**Do I need to apply for the Principal Residence Exemption, or is it
automatic?**
You generally need to file it with your local assessor — it isn't applied
automatically just because you live in the home.

**Are property taxes included in my mortgage payment?**
Usually yes — most lenders collect a portion of your annual property tax
each month through an escrow account, then pay the bill on your behalf
when it's due.
