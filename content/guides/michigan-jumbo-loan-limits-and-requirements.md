A **jumbo loan** is any mortgage that's larger than the conforming loan
limit set annually by the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA). Almost
every county in Michigan uses the standard, baseline conforming limit
rather than the higher limits set for expensive coastal metro areas —
Michigan doesn't have any FHFA-designated high-cost counties. That means
the point where a Michigan loan tips into "jumbo" territory is generally
the same statewide.

## Why the limit matters

Conforming loans can be sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, which keeps
their rates and underwriting fairly standardized across lenders. Jumbo
loans fall outside that system, so lenders hold more of the risk
themselves — which is why jumbo underwriting tends to be stricter.

Because the conforming limit is updated annually, the exact current
dollar threshold is worth confirming directly with a lender or on
FHFA's website rather than relying on a number that may be out of date
by the time you're shopping.

## How jumbo underwriting typically differs

Requirements vary by lender, but jumbo loans commonly involve:

- **Higher credit score expectations** — often meaningfully above what's
  needed for a conforming conventional loan
- **Larger down payments** — many jumbo programs expect at least
  10-20% down, though this varies by lender and loan size
- **Cash reserve requirements** — proof you have several months of
  mortgage payments in savings beyond your down payment and closing
  costs
- **Tighter debt-to-income limits** — less flexibility than some
  conforming programs allow
- **Sometimes two appraisals** — especially on larger loan amounts, as
  an extra layer of value verification

## Where this actually comes up in Michigan

Most Michigan home prices fall comfortably under the conforming limit,
so jumbo financing mostly comes up for larger homes in higher-priced
markets — parts of Oakland County, some lakefront and waterfront
properties, and new construction on the higher end. It's not a loan
type most Michigan buyers will need, but it's worth knowing if you're
shopping in that price range.

## What to do if you're right at the line

If your target home price puts you close to the conforming limit:

- A slightly larger down payment can sometimes keep your loan amount
  under the conforming threshold, avoiding jumbo underwriting entirely
- Ask your lender to confirm the current-year conforming limit before
  you assume either way — it's a fixed number, not an estimate
- Compare actual quotes — jumbo rates aren't automatically worse than
  conforming rates, and the gap depends on market conditions and the
  specific lender

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a jumbo loan always require 20% down?**
Not always — some jumbo programs go lower, but requirements vary
significantly more by lender than conforming loan down payments do, so
it's worth shopping more than one lender.

**Can I get an FHA jumbo loan?**
FHA has its own separate loan limit, and in Michigan that limit sits at
or near the standard conforming limit in nearly every county, so an
"FHA jumbo" is rarely relevant here the way it can be in high-cost
states.

**Is jumbo mortgage insurance required like it is on conventional
loans?**
It depends on the lender and loan structure — some jumbo programs don't
require mortgage insurance even above 80% loan-to-value, while others
do. This varies enough that it's worth asking directly rather than
assuming either way.
