MSHDA sets an income limit on its down payment assistance and MI Home Loan
programs, but that limit isn't one flat number for the whole state. It
varies by **county** and by **household size**, and it changes when MSHDA
updates its program guidelines. Here's how the structure works and where
to find the actual current figures.

## Why the limit isn't the same everywhere

MSHDA ties its income limits to area median income (AMI) data, which
differs by county and metro area. A higher cost-of-living county will
generally carry a higher income limit than a lower cost-of-living one.
On top of that, MSHDA designates certain **targeted areas** — specific
census tracts, often in areas the state is trying to encourage
investment in — that carry higher income limits (and higher purchase
price limits) than the rest of the county.

## What counts as "household income"

MSHDA generally counts the income of everyone who will live in the home,
not just the borrowers on the loan. That's a common point of confusion:
a non-borrowing spouse's or family member's income can still count
toward the household total even if they're not on the mortgage.

## Why we're not listing specific numbers here

MSHDA updates its income limits periodically, and they differ for every
one of Michigan's 83 counties plus targeted-area exceptions within many
of them. A table frozen at today's numbers would go stale and could
send you toward the wrong figure for your county. Instead:

- Your loan officer or MSHDA-participating lender can pull the exact
  current limit for your county and household size in a couple of
  minutes
- MSHDA's own website publishes the current limits directly

## What to do if you're close to the limit

If your household income is near the cutoff, a few things are worth
knowing before you assume you don't qualify:

- Limits are based on your *current* household income, not projected
  future income
- The limit depends on the county the **property** is in, not where you
  currently live — buying in a neighboring county can sometimes mean a
  different limit
- Targeted areas within your county may carry a higher limit than the
  county's standard figure

## Frequently asked questions

**Does MSHDA use my gross income or net income?**
Gross income — before taxes and deductions — is the standard basis for
these calculations, consistent with how most mortgage income
calculations work.

**If I'm over the limit for MSHDA assistance, am I locked out of all
Michigan homebuyer help?**
No — income limits are specific to MSHDA's programs. Other assistance,
like some city-run programs, sets its own separate limits, so it's worth
checking more than one source rather than assuming one limit applies
everywhere.

**Do income limits change every year?**
They're updated periodically as area median income data is refreshed,
not necessarily on a fixed annual schedule, so it's worth confirming the
current figure close to when you actually apply rather than relying on
a number you saw months earlier.
