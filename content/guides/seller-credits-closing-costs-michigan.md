Yes — sellers can contribute toward your closing costs in Michigan, and
it's a common, normal part of negotiating an offer, not an unusual ask.
This is called a **seller concession** or **seller credit**.

## How it works

Instead of, or in addition to, negotiating the purchase price down, you
ask the seller to contribute a set dollar amount toward your closing
costs. That amount gets credited at closing, reducing the cash you need
to bring.

Importantly, this isn't cash handed to you — it flows directly into
reducing your closing cost bill, and it's documented as part of the
purchase agreement and disclosed to the lender.

## The limits, by loan type

Loan programs cap how much a seller can contribute, expressed as a
percentage of the purchase price:

- **Conventional loans**: typically 3% if your down payment is under 10%,
  up to 6% if your down payment is 10% or more, and higher again above
  25% down — the exact tier depends on your specific down payment amount
- **FHA loans**: generally capped around 6%
- **VA loans**: closing cost concessions are generally unlimited, though
  a separate 4% cap applies to certain other seller-paid items
- **USDA loans**: generally capped around 6%

These caps apply to the *combined* total of seller-paid closing costs —
you can't stack multiple credits past the limit for your loan type.

## When sellers are more willing to offer this

Seller concessions are far more common in buyer-favorable markets — when
homes are sitting longer, or in price ranges with more competition among
sellers. In a hot, multiple-offer market, asking for a large concession
can make your offer less competitive compared to one without that ask.

## A practical alternative: split the difference

If a seller is unwilling to reduce price but has some room, some buyers
negotiate a portion as a price reduction and a portion as a closing cost
credit — this can be worth discussing with your real estate agent based
on the specific home and market conditions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a seller credit affect my loan amount?**
No — the credit reduces your cash needed at closing; it doesn't change
what you're borrowing or your monthly payment.

**Can a seller credit exceed my actual closing costs?**
No — lenders only allow the credit to be applied up to your actual
closing costs. Any amount beyond that isn't paid out to you or applied
elsewhere.

**Do I need to ask for this in my initial offer, or can I negotiate it
later?**
It's typically written directly into the purchase offer itself, though it
can sometimes be renegotiated later — for example, if an inspection turns
up issues, buyers sometimes ask for an added credit at that stage.
