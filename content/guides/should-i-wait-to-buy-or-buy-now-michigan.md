There's no reliable way to predict where mortgage rates or home prices
will go over the next six or twelve months — anyone claiming certainty
about that is guessing. So instead of trying to time the market, it's
more useful to work through the factors that are actually knowable about
your own situation.

## Questions that matter more than market timing

### How long do you plan to stay?
Buying costs (closing costs, moving expenses) get spread out over however
long you own the home. A shorter expected stay makes those upfront costs
weigh more heavily; a longer expected stay dilutes them. If you might
move again in a year or two, that's a bigger factor than what rates do
next quarter.

### Does the payment work today, at today's rate?
If you can comfortably afford a home at current rates, waiting for a
hoped-for rate drop carries real risk: rates could just as easily move
the other way, or prices could rise faster than a rate drop would have
saved you. If today's payment doesn't work, that's a real constraint —
not one to solve by guessing about future rates.

### Is your down payment and reserve fund solid?
Buying before you have a stable emergency fund on top of your down
payment can leave you financially stretched the moment something
unexpected comes up — a job change, a repair, a medical bill.

### What does your local market actually look like right now?
Michigan's market varies a lot by region and season — competitive
metro-area neighborhoods behave differently than slower-moving rural
markets, and spring/summer tends to bring more listings and more
competition than winter. Talking to a local agent about current
inventory and competition in your specific target area is more useful
than reading general national headlines.

## The cost of waiting vs. the cost of buying now

| | Buying now | Waiting |
|---|---|---|
| Rate | Locks in today's rate — refinance later if rates drop | Unknown — could be better or worse |
| Price | Locks in today's price | Unknown — could be higher or lower |
| Rent (if renting) | Stops accumulating | Continues, with no equity built |
| Flexibility | Less — committed to a home | More — can wait for the right listing or moment |

Refinancing is a real option if rates drop meaningfully after you buy —
it's part of why "waiting for a better rate" isn't always the safer
choice it feels like. Waiting for a better *price*, by contrast, isn't
something you can undo later the same way.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it ever clearly better to wait?**
Sometimes — if your finances genuinely aren't ready (thin savings,
unstable income, credit that needs work), waiting to strengthen your
position is usually the right call regardless of what the market is
doing.

**Should I try to time a rate drop before buying?**
It's generally not a reliable strategy, since rate movements aren't
predictable. Many buyers instead buy when the payment works for them
and plan to refinance later if rates improve.

**Does Michigan have a "best" season to buy?**
Spring and summer typically bring more inventory but also more
competition; fall and winter tend to have less inventory but less
competition too. Neither is universally "better" — it depends on
whether you value more options or less competition more.
