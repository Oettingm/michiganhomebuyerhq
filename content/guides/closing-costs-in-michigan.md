Most Michigan buyers pay somewhere between 2% and 5% of the home's purchase
price in closing costs. On a $275,000 home, that's roughly $5,500 to
$13,750 — on top of your down payment.

That range is wide because closing costs are really a bundle of separate
fees, and which ones apply to you depends on your loan type, your lender,
and which county you're buying in.

## What's actually in "closing costs"

### Lender fees
Origination charges, underwriting, and processing fees. These vary by
lender, which is part of why shopping more than one quote matters.

### Third-party fees
Appraisal, title insurance, title search, survey, and recording fees. These
are largely set by the market, not the lender, so they don't move much
between quotes.

### Prepaid items
Homeowners insurance (first year, paid upfront), property tax escrow, and
prepaid interest. These aren't really a "cost" of the loan — you'd pay
insurance and taxes either way — but they do show up as cash due at
closing.

### Michigan-specific line items
Michigan charges a state transfer tax, and most counties add their own
county transfer tax on top. Who pays this (buyer or seller) is negotiable
and often set by local custom rather than law, so it's worth asking early
rather than assuming.

## Can the seller help cover this?

Yes — seller concessions are common in Michigan, especially in
buyer-favorable markets. FHA, VA, and conventional loans each cap how much
a seller can contribute as a percentage of the purchase price, and the cap
depends on your down payment size for conventional loans.

## A rough example

| Home price | Estimated closing costs (3%) |
|---|---|
| $200,000 | $6,000 |
| $300,000 | $9,000 |
| $400,000 | $12,000 |

These are planning estimates, not a quote — your actual number depends on
your loan program, lender, and county.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do closing costs come out of my down payment?**
No — they're separate. You need your down payment and your closing costs
as two distinct amounts of cash at closing, unless you've arranged seller
credits or lender credits to offset part of it.

**Can I roll closing costs into the loan?**
On a purchase, generally no — closing costs are typically paid in cash or
covered by credits, not financed into the loan balance, with some
exceptions on certain refinance transactions.

**Are closing costs the same everywhere in Michigan?**
No. County transfer tax rates and local recording fees vary, so a home in
Wayne County and a home in Kent County won't land at exactly the same
closing cost total even at the same price.
