There's no single "best" loan type — the right one depends on your credit,
your down payment savings, and where in Michigan you're buying. Here's how
the three most common options actually compare.

## Conventional

- **Down payment**: as low as 3% for qualified first-time buyers, though
  3-5% is more typical
- **Credit**: generally needs stronger credit than FHA to get the best
  terms
- **Mortgage insurance**: required if you put down less than 20%, but it
  automatically cancels once you reach 20% equity — unlike FHA
- **Best fit for**: buyers with solid credit and at least a modest down
  payment saved, who want mortgage insurance that eventually goes away

## FHA

- **Down payment**: as low as 3.5%
- **Credit**: more forgiving of lower credit scores than conventional
  loans
- **Mortgage insurance**: required for the life of the loan in most cases
  (unless you refinance out of FHA later), which is the main tradeoff
  against conventional
- **Best fit for**: buyers with limited savings or a shorter/imperfect
  credit history who need more flexible qualifying standards

## USDA / Rural Development

- **Down payment**: 0% — no down payment required
- **Credit**: moderate flexibility, similar territory to FHA
- **Location restriction**: the property has to be in a USDA-eligible
  rural or suburban area — this rules out much of Metro Detroit but covers
  a surprising amount of the rest of the state
- **Income limits**: household income has to fall under USDA's limit for
  the area
- **Best fit for**: buyers targeting an eligible area who want to avoid a
  down payment entirely

## A side-by-side snapshot

| | Conventional | FHA | USDA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Min. down payment | ~3% | 3.5% | 0% |
| Mortgage insurance | Cancels at 20% equity | Usually life of loan | Ongoing fee, generally lower than FHA |
| Location restriction | None | None | Eligible areas only |
| Income limits | None | None | Yes |

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I switch loan types partway through the process?**
Sometimes, though it can affect your timeline and your rate lock, so it's
better to settle on the right fit as early as possible rather than
switching midstream.

**Does a lower down payment always mean a worse deal?**
Not necessarily — it depends on your specific financial picture. A 0%
USDA loan can be the stronger choice for the right buyer, even compared
to putting money down elsewhere.

**Is FHA only for first-time buyers?**
No — FHA loans are available to repeat buyers too, not just
first-timers, contrary to a common assumption.
