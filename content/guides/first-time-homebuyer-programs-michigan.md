Michigan has more first-time buyer support available than most people
realize, spread across state programs, local city programs, and loan-type
specific options. Here's the landscape.

## State-level: MSHDA programs

The **Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA)** runs the
state's primary homebuyer assistance:

- **MI Home Loan** — for first-time buyers statewide, and repeat buyers
  in targeted areas, with down payment assistance built in
- **MI Home Loan Flex** — opens the program to repeat buyers statewide,
  not just targeted areas
- Down payment assistance up to $10,000 (or $12,500 in some targeted
  areas), structured as a 0%-interest second loan

See the full MSHDA down payment assistance guide for how the assistance
itself works.

## City and local programs

Several Michigan cities run their own homebuyer assistance on top of, or
separate from, state programs — these often have narrower eligibility
(you typically need to buy within city limits) but can stack with MSHDA
in some cases. Examples include down payment and closing cost assistance
programs run by cities like Grand Rapids, Lansing, and Warren, each with
their own income limits and specific terms. Since these change and vary
significantly by city, it's worth checking directly with your target
city's housing or community development department.

## Loan-type specific advantages

Beyond dedicated assistance programs, the loan type itself can function
as a form of buyer support:

- **FHA loans** — lower credit and down payment thresholds
- **USDA loans** — 0% down in eligible rural and suburban areas
- **VA loans** — 0% down for eligible veterans and service members, with
  no ongoing mortgage insurance

## How these typically combine

A common pattern for Michigan first-time buyers: an FHA or conventional
loan, paired with MSHDA down payment assistance, sometimes stacked with a
city program if buying within an eligible city's limits. Not every
combination is possible — eligibility depends on the specific programs
involved — but it's worth exploring more than one option rather than
assuming only one applies to you.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to be a first-time buyer to use MSHDA assistance?**
Not always — the MI Home Loan Flex program is open to repeat buyers
statewide; the standard MI Home Loan program has first-time buyer
requirements in most areas, with an exception for targeted areas.

**Can I combine a city program with MSHDA assistance?**
Sometimes — it depends on the specific city program's rules. It's worth
asking directly rather than assuming either way.

**Is "first-time buyer" defined the same way across all these
programs?**
Not necessarily — many programs define it as not having owned a home in
the past three years, rather than literally never having owned one, but
the exact definition can vary by program.
